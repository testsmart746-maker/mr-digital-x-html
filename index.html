Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common profile and demo questions.

How long should my LinkedIn summary (About) section be?

Aim for 3–5 short paragraphs, roughly 200–300 words. Open with a strong hook about who you are and what you build. Include your core skills (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Python), a couple of project achievements, and a clear call to action — for example "Open to freelance projects and full-time opportunities."

LinkedIn only shows the first two lines before the "see more" click, so make those count.

What tools can I use to record screen demos?

On Windows, the built-in Xbox Game Bar (Win + G) records any window for free. OBS Studio is free and more powerful. On Mac, QuickTime Player records screens natively. For mobile recordings, most Android and iOS versions have a built-in screen recorder in the notification shade or Control Centre.

Keep recordings to 20–40 seconds, narrate what you're doing or add captions, and export at 1080p if possible.

How do I host a live demo for free?

GitHub Pages is the simplest option for static HTML/CSS/JS projects — push your repo, enable Pages in Settings, and you get a free URL in seconds. Netlify and Vercel both offer free tiers with automatic deployments from GitHub. For Python projects, Railway and Render have free starter plans.

Should I connect with people I don't know on LinkedIn?

Yes — LinkedIn is a professional networking platform, not a personal social network. Sending a short personalised note with your connection request ("Hi [Name], I'm a web developer building projects in HTML/CSS/JS and Python. I'd love to connect with you.") dramatically increases acceptance rates and starts a real conversation.

Target recruiters at companies you admire, founders of startups in your area, and developers a few years ahead of you in their career.

How often should I update my profile?

Add each new project to your Featured section as soon as it's live. Update your Experience or Projects sections every 1–2 months as your skills and work evolve. LinkedIn's algorithm surfaces active profiles, so even small updates (a new skill endorsement, a fresh post) help keep you visible.
